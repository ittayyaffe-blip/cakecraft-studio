# Recipe Guide — Maison de Gâteau Paris

## Cake Flavors
- **Vanilla** — a Madagascar bourbon vanilla bean sponge, moist and dense enough to support fondant or tall stacked tiers. The house default; pairs with every filling and frosting.
- **Chocolate** — a Belgian dark cocoa sponge, deep and slightly bitter to balance sweet fillings. Popular for Birthday and Corporate orders.
- **Red Velvet** — a cocoa-and-buttermilk sponge with a natural beet-based red tint (no artificial dye), traditionally paired with Cream Cheese frosting. A frequent Wedding and Graduation choice.

## Fillings
- **Strawberry** — fresh strawberry compote, made in-house, folded with a light pastry cream. Best with Vanilla or Red Velvet sponge.
- **Chocolate Ganache** — dark chocolate and cream, poured warm and set between layers for a dense, rich bite. Pairs naturally with Chocolate sponge.
- **Lemon Curd** — hand-whisked lemon curd, bright and tart, a deliberate contrast against sweeter frostings. A common Baby Shower and Graduation request for a lighter-tasting cake.

## Frostings
- **Buttercream** — the house standard, a European-style buttercream (less sweet than American buttercream), holds shape well for piped decoration and is the most weather-stable option for outdoor Wedding or Corporate events.
- **Cream Cheese** — traditional pairing for Red Velvet, softer and more perishable than buttercream; not recommended for cakes that will sit unrefrigerated more than 3 hours (see Food Safety Procedures).
- **Dark Chocolate Ganache** — poured or whipped as an exterior finish, the most heat-stable of the three frostings, preferred for summer outdoor events.

## Sizing
Three sizes are offered: **Small** (8–10 servings), **Medium**, and **Large**. Larger sizes take proportionally more bake and cooling time, not just more batter — a Large order should be started at least one production slot earlier than a Small of the same design complexity.

## Substitutions
Flavor, filling, and frosting can be mixed freely across all five collections and templates — there is no fixed pairing requirement, only the pairings noted above as head-baker recommendations for taste and structural stability.
